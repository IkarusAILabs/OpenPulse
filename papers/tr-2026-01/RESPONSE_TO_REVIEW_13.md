# Response to the thirteenth review: TR-2026-01

We checked every point against the draft and, where the point concerns behaviour, against the code of release v0.8.0 (measured at commit `787beb8`; the release adds only the version number). The review recommends **Reject** for a full research paper at IEEE S&P, ACM CCS, USENIX Security or NDSS in its current form. It calls the report a valuable technical report and a credible foundation for further research.

## Overall

We accept the recommendation and its reason. The paper shows a specification-and-audit discipline, but not yet that its distinctive completeness model gives a practical security benefit over careful existing approaches. The six issues of the summary are:
1. the completeness mechanism is not empirically isolated;
2. trusted declarations and identity mappings are the main unmeasured determinants of negative soundness;
3. the membership specification does not treat named examples, roles and identity-based no-association in one place;
4. the main comparator is weak and was defined inconsistently;
5. the retrospective study cannot support population-level conclusions;
6. discovery, advisory matching, consumption safety and sustained monitoring are not fully evaluated.

Issues 1, 2, 5 and 6 cannot be closed by revising text. They need independent annotators, a held-out evaluation, an advisory benchmark or a monitored population. Issues 3 and 4 are specification and protocol problems, and this revision fixes them in the text.

We also took the summary's advice to narrow the paper. It now centres the typed applicability model and its audit, describes one tagged release, OpenPulse v0.8.0, and keeps historical states to the audit method and the artifact manifest. Most of the prospective protocol, the development ledger and the unimplemented designs have moved to appendices or to `evaluation/FURTHER_STUDIES.md`.

What this revision does:
- it makes the text match the snapshot and the evidence, and states the review's conditional security claim as the paper's own;
- it restructures the paper (item 1.10);
- it amends the registration draft (`evaluation/REGISTRATION.md`) for item 1.6, and corrects one sentence in `evaluation/retrospective/REPORT.md`.

**No code was changed in this revision.** Two new code findings (D16 and a latent package/source-repository gap) are described in the paper as open, not fixed. The open items are listed under "Not changed, and why".

**Length.** Main text, from the title page through the AI-assistance statement: about 25,500 words before and about 20,700 after (−19%). At about 580 words per page that is **45 → about 36 pages**. This is an estimate: CI has not built the PDF yet. The appendices grew from about 2,500 to about 7,800 words, so the whole PDF stays at roughly its former 54 pages. The target in our own plan for 1.10 was about 31 main-text pages. We missed it because the other items added required content: the conditional claim, per-dependency freshness, the purpose attribute, the aggregate-reporting requirements, the cost measurement and several retrospective paragraphs.
